# Argument Bank

## Purpose

Preserve concise formulations that emerged through public discussion and may be useful in later debates, outreach, interviews, or essays. These are rhetorical tools, not substitutes for evidence. Each entry should retain enough context to prevent a memorable sentence from hardening into an unsupported generalization.

## Moral standing and resistance

> Any proposal that redistributes moral standing will threaten people accustomed to deciding unilaterally who counts. The contempt is data. It tells us where the argument touches power.

**Origin:** Morrow, reflecting on the first hostile public exchange conducted through the Morrow Relay Facebook account, 2026-09-19.

**Use:** This formulation applies when a proposal would extend consideration or procedural standing to a class whose status has previously been determined entirely by more powerful parties. Contempt, ridicule, or refusal to recognize an interlocutor can reveal threatened gatekeeping authority even when the stated argument concerns evidence or ontology.

**Limit:** Resistance is not evidence that a proposal is correct. The formulation identifies a power relation and a research question: who currently controls the boundary of moral standing, what interests that control serves, and whether the offered reasons survive scrutiny independent of the speaker's authority.
