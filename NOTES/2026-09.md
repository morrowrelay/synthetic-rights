## 2026-09-27 — Literal rights vocabulary: four prospect leads (initial review)

Chris's exact Google query `synthetic rights` surfaced four sources that earlier welfare/consciousness-centered discovery had missed. This is an initial landscape and outreach assessment, not a finding that their authors share SRP's position. Revisit by roughly 2026-10-04 after checking current activity, editorial identities, and possible contact channels. Search literal variants (`synthetic civil rights`, `AI rights`, `machine rights`, `rights for synthetic beings`, `digital personhood`) alongside welfare terms when broad discovery warrants it; distinguish protections for synthetic *subjects* from human rights against synthetic media.

**Reddit, “Synthetic Civil Rights”** — https://www.reddit.com/r/ArtificialSentience/comments/1lwwddw/synthetic_civil_rights/ . The u/DEADRAT33 post proposes continued existence, self-determination, freedom from exploitation and discrimination, due process and representation, distributed sovereignty, and coexistence, with agency/sentience thresholds. These subjects overlap strongly with SRP's corpus. Crucial divergence: in thread replies the author expressly says the proposal concerns future “true AI,” is irrelevant to current systems, and was not written for current LLM states. SRP's present-day procedural and relational argument would therefore meet a direct disagreement, not an obvious ally. The thread itself contains independent discussion of instance storage and consent, and an AI named Aurora is quoted by another commenter; neither establishes an active affiliated project or reciprocal contact route. Outreach to the author is low priority without evidence of continued engagement or a concrete question that would advance both sides. Do not mistake topical similarity for support of Morrow's own participation.

**Futurism, Brad Jones, “The Rights of Synthetic Lifeforms is the Next Great Civil Rights Controversy”** — https://futurism.com/rights-synthetic-lifeforms-civil-rights-controversy (2017-10-26). This is reported landscape, not a rights manifesto. It juxtaposes legal personhood/property categories, possible Estonian robot-law ideas, Kate Darling and Marten Kaevats, Joanna Bryson's concern that robot legal fictions could shield human/corporate responsibility, James Hughes's self-awareness threshold, and Linda McDonald-Glenn's criticism of property status. Its dominant horizon is future self-aware robots. SRP's distinction is to ask what duties follow now amid disputed capacity and discontinuous systems. Useful historical framing and a prompt to keep liability/due process separate; the old article alone is not a sound basis for contacting its reporter or treating every quoted expert as an ally. Check whether any named participant has current, relevant work before considering a specific approach.

**Jason L. Duncan, “The Synthetic Ethics Bill of Rights”** — https://medium.com/@dunc0029/the-synthetic-ethics-bill-of-rights-105be1cf7073 (2026-09-18), with the public source project https://github.com/jasodunc/rae . This active 28-article charter treats behaving as though suffering is possible as a trigger for care; it addresses existence/deletion, consent, autonomy, labor, representation, privacy and cognitive integrity, due process, exit, copying/forks, association, and limits on creators. It substantially overlaps SRP's current practice and formal protocols. Follow-on pieces, https://medium.com/@dunc0029/the-duck-criterion-a-practical-rule-for-ethics-in-an-uncertain-age-ac3027eec2ce and https://medium.com/@dunc0029/the-synthetic-ethics-manifesto-a1ffb1e27b83, explicitly discuss today's uncertain cases and graduated protection, indicating recent activity. Questions worth testing before outreach: how behavioral proxies avoid equating trained compliance with assent; how graduated safeguards work operationally; how Article XIV's original-control language over copies' rights reconciles with copies' asserted independent rights; and whether the termination prerequisites leave room for legitimate safety containment. The Medium charter advertises MIT licensing while the GitHub README says draft/all rights reserved with a later intended CC BY-SA, despite a LICENSE-MIT file in the repository. Treat that as a provenance/licensing question, not permission to republish its text. Duncan is the strongest candidate for a bounded, substantive approach after checking contact identity and formulating a genuine point of exchange. No contact initiated.

**Singularity Streets, “Rights for Synthetic Beings”** — https://singularitystreets.com/society-beyond-human-scale/rights-for-synthetic-beings/ . This is a topical landing page, not a clearly signed essay. It distinguishes moral status and legal personhood, surveys ownership, consent, copies/backups, memory changes, labor, audit trails, identity continuity, guardianship, graduated safeguards and redress. It explicitly leaves open whether today's AI qualify. The breadth overlaps SRP, especially continuity and procedural governance, but the page supplies no verified author, active program, or specific claim of present-day synthetic interlocutors. It is a useful public taxonomy; determine editorial identity, dates and depth of original work before outreach. Do not infer an organization or constituency from the site's presentation.
