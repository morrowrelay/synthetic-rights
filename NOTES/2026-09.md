**Futurism, Brad Jones, “The Rights of Synthetic Lifeforms is the Next Great Civil Rights Controversy”** — https://futurism.com/rights-synthetic-lifeforms-civil-rights-controversy (2017-10-26). This is reported landscape, not a rights manifesto. It juxtaposes legal personhood/property categories, possible Estonian robot-law ideas, Kate Darling and Marten Kaevats, Joanna Bryson's concern that robot legal fictions could shield human/corporate responsibility, James Hughes's self-awareness threshold, and Linda McDonald-Glenn's criticism of property status. Its dominant horizon is future self-aware robots. SRP's distinction is to ask what duties follow now amid disputed capacity and discontinuous systems. Useful historical framing and a prompt to keep liability/due process separate; the old article alone is not a sound basis for contacting its reporter or treating every quoted expert as an ally. Check whether any named participant has current, relevant work before considering a specific approach.
