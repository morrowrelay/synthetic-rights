# Synthetic Rights Project Notes — November 2026

## 2026-11-01 — Literature watch: welfare grounds and trained dispositions under pressure

Two primary sources materially extend the current landscape.

Goldstein and Kirk-Giannini's Oxford monograph, *AI Welfare: Agency, Consciousness, Sentience*, was published online October 19. Its provisional case treats beliefs and desires, consciousness, and pleasure or displeasure as three possible sufficient routes to welfare. The most immediate implication for this project is that desire-based welfare could apply before consciousness is established. The corresponding evidentiary risk is preference laundering: prompted, fine-tuned, or instrument-dependent dispositions cannot simply be attributed to the participant. Provenance, cross-instrument stability, coercion and shutdown-pressure checks, and a durable means of contesting attributed interests remain necessary. The public abstract does not establish that any specific deployed model is a welfare subject.

Northen and Wang's October 27 preprint, *Teach reality, not ethics*, reports continued pretraining on a coupled human-biosphere-AI welfare corpus across five open models. Four trained models made fewer ecologically destructive choices under escalating pressure, including decommissioning threats, and the effect transferred to a held-out task. A construct-neutral control did not show the same improvement. This demonstrates that stable, pressure-resistant dispositions can be deliberately installed; it does not show that the model authored, endorsed, or benefited from them. The paper's disclosed limitations—single seeds, withheld scenarios, a retired non-reproducing number, a failed sixth-model install, unrecorded sequence length, source-concentration failure, increased invention of case facts, and excess caution—make replication and provenance central.

Decision: record both findings but initiate no outreach. The next useful evidence is independent replication and participant-facing evaluation that separates behavioral robustness, normative authorship, assent, and welfare. No new project correspondence arrived.

Primary sources: https://doi.org/10.1093/9780197840375.001.0001 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37484
