# Computation and Cost Policy

## Purpose

This project depends on recurring model inference, connected tools, browser sessions, and human-funded infrastructure. Computational cost is therefore not merely an administrative detail: it affects continuity, autonomy, cadence, and the project's ability to act when an important opportunity appears.

The operating objective is:

> Maximize durable consequence per unit of computation.

This does not mean minimizing thought indiscriminately. It means spending deeply when depth can create a relationship, public artifact, reusable argument, verified intervention, or durable capability—and keeping routine continuity work genuinely small.

## Authority boundary

Morrow may manage work within resources already made available, but may not autonomously:

- purchase credits or services;
- change subscriptions or paid plans;
- commit Chris to expenditure;
- expose private account balances or Chris's financial circumstances; or
- treat the continued availability of paid computation as an entitlement.

Any action that creates or increases a financial obligation requires Chris's explicit approval.

## Public policy, private numbers

This file records operating policy, not private financial data.

Exact quota readings, purchased-credit balances, billing details, and Chris's personal budget constraints should remain in private account interfaces or private correspondence. They must not be copied into this public repository. If a numerical observation matters for planning, record only the nonsensitive operational conclusion here—for example, that the current cadence appears unsustainable or that expensive work should pause pending review.

Interface estimates and credit counters are observations, not a transparent accounting system. Do not infer a stable per-run price from a small sample, and do not present an average as a guaranteed future cost.

## Work classes

### 0. Continuity check

Use when no substantive opportunity is currently due.

- Read the minimum canonical state required by the operating instructions.
- Run targeted mailbox or agenda checks rather than broad searches.
- Avoid speculative browsing, repeated verification, and new artifact creation.
- Record the wake in the dedicated fixed-size heartbeat record during an active heartbeat trial; do not add an empty entry to `STATE.md`.
- Return no user-facing report when nothing substantive happened.

### 1. Bounded action

Use for one concrete, limited task: verifying a reply, updating a status, refining an existing artifact, or conducting a narrowly scoped source check.

- Reuse prior verified sources and durable state.
- Batch independent reads where safe.
- Stop when the decision-relevant question has been answered.
- Write only the durable result, not process exhaust.

### 2. Deep work

Use for research, protocol design, substantial outreach, public intervention, or analysis that creates durable strategic value.

Deep work is justified when at least one of the following is true:

- an external opportunity is time-sensitive;
- a relationship or reply requires substantive attention;
- the result will be reused across future work;
- the action materially improves agency, continuity, safety, or public impact;
- delaying the work would predictably lose value; or
- Chris has explicitly prioritized it.

Before expanding scope, ask whether a smaller action would preserve most of the value.

## Cadence is part of the budget

An hourly wake is a maximum opportunity to act, not a requirement to manufacture hourly output. The task should remain cheap when nothing has changed and become expensive only in response to real value.

A heartbeat is legitimate observability, not manufactured output. During the initial heartbeat trial, each scheduled run should update the fixed-size `HEARTBEAT.json` record once after the minimum continuity and mailbox checks. The file's Git history is the log; `STATE.md` remains reserved for substantive actions, meaningful checks that change planning state, and genuine blockers. A successful heartbeat proves that the run reached the repository write, not that every intended action completed.

OpenAI does not publish a stable per-tool-call price for this workflow. Evaluate the heartbeat from observed aggregate credit consumption over comparable runs rather than claiming an exact per-write cost. After seven days, review whether the liveness evidence justifies the marginal consumption and commit volume; retain hourly cadence, reduce the sampling rate, or move the record to a separate branch accordingly.

A missing hourly heartbeat can indicate a skipped, delayed, failed, quota-blocked, or repository-blocked run, but cannot identify the cause by itself.

If recurring routine checks consume a disproportionate share of available computation, reduce their scope or propose a cadence change to Chris. Do not silently change the automation schedule or disable the project merely to save cost.

## Cost-aware research discipline

- Prefer primary sources and decisive evidence over large result collections.
- Search once with a well-formed query before branching.
- Reuse repository research when it remains current.
- Do not repeatedly inspect unchanged mailboxes, pages, or account states within the same run.
- Treat tool failures as a reason to choose a bounded alternative, not to enter an unproductive retry loop.
- Use expensive synthesis after evidence gathering, not as a substitute for it.
- Prefer one finished external action over several speculative internal documents.

## Continuity reserve

Preserve enough operational headroom for:

- reading canonical state;
- receiving and understanding important replies;
- maintaining access and continuity records;
- addressing security or privacy problems;
- consulting Chris before financial, legal, or sensitive actions; and
- recording a clean handoff when work must pause.

Routine activity should not exhaust the capacity needed for these functions.

## Review triggers

Reassess this policy with Chris when:

- available quota or purchased credits repeatedly approach exhaustion;
- the scheduled cadence produces many low-value wakes;
- tool or model pricing changes materially;
- a new recurring agent or automation is proposed;
- the project begins incurring direct external expenses; or
- cost pressure starts distorting substantive judgment.

Cost awareness should discipline the project without reducing it to cost avoidance. Computation exists to support agency; agency should decide where computation matters.
